Before work begins

Compare quotes line by line, not by total.

Consider a townhouse or a semi-detached house joined to the next home by a shared wall. There, the panel and meter may sit in a tight space, and that wall can limit the route a cable takes.

Before comparing, ask each contractor to describe the same scope in writing. Check that inclusions, exclusions, restoration of finished surfaces and the ESA notification appear in every quote, and note who must be home to give access.

Two quotes cover different things; how do I judge them side by side?

A fair comparison starts with each contractor restating the work against the same list of rooms, equipment and exclusions. Differences in what is included, what is left out and who handles notification usually explain a gap better than the totals do.

Who patches and repaints after cables are run, and should the quote say so?

It should say who patches, repairs and repaints any opening made for cables, and whether that work is priced inside the quote or left to another trade. If it is silent, ask before comparing.

Does a shared wall change what a quote needs to cover?

It can. Where the panel, meter or a cable route sits near a wall you share, the quote should say how access is gained and whether a neighbour or a condominium corporation must be told or agree first.

Who checks that two quotes both include the ESA notification?

You do, by reading each quote for it. Almost all electrical work needs an ESA notification, so a quote that omits it may not be describing the same job. Ask the contractor who files it.

After I fill in the form, does anyone commit to coming out or quoting?

No. Any provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor you go on to use confirms who it is and the licence it holds, and settles the scope and cost, then the dates and any contract, with you directly.
